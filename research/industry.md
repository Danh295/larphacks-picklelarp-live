# Tennis and pickleball market: research notes

Source quality: most figures come from search-result summaries, not from opening the primary PDFs. Each bullet lists the URL the figure came from. Anything not confirmed in a primary source is marked **unverified**.

## Participation (US, 2025 activity)

- Pickleball: 24.3M players in 2025, up 22.8% YoY; about 4.5M new players; 7.48M "core" players (8+ times/yr), up 20.4%. Source: SFIA 2026 Topline Participation Report, https://sfia.org/research/u-s-pickleball-participation/ (summary via https://pickleball.com/news/sfia-report-confirms-over-24-million-americans-playing-pickleball)
- Tennis: 27.3M players in 2025, up 6.2% YoY (sixth straight year of growth). Source: USTA 2026 US Tennis Participation Report, https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf
- Tennis new players: about 4.9M first-time players in 2025, an all-time high (up ~9%); 1.7M returning players; 80% of 2024 players played again in 2025 (five-year high). Source: https://www.usta.com/en/home/stay-current/national/tennis-participation-continues-to-surge-with-six-consecutive-yea.html
- Caveat: the two counts use different methodologies (SFIA and USTA both draw on Physical Activity Council-style data but are not the same survey). Treat the 24.3M vs 27.3M comparison as indicative only.
- Older pickleball figure: APP 2023 study put US pickleball players at 48.3M (YouGov, 12-month recall, adults). Source: https://thedinkpickleball.com/app-participation-report-2023 (secondary). Methodology differs sharply from SFIA's; the gap is not reconciled.
- ITF global tennis: about 106M players worldwide in the 2024 Global Tennis Report, up from 84.4M in 2019; female share 40.3%, down from 47% in 2019. Source: https://www.itftennis.com/en/news-and-media/articles/itf-global-tennis-report-now-available-on-itf-academy/ (figures via https://tennistourtalk.com/116526/itf-global-tennis-report-reveals-worldwide-tennis-participation-surges-to-106-million, secondary)
- No global pickleball total found. **Unverified.**

## Demographics

- Women are 42.9% of US pickleball players (up from 38.6% in 2020). Source: SFIA 2026 report via the sources above. Primary PDF not opened.
- Average pickleball player age 34.8 (down from 41 in 2020). Source: https://www.bounce.game/blog/pickleball-statistics (secondary). **Unverified** against SFIA.
- Tennis: adults 35+ drove about 95% of 2025 growth; youth participation is still below its 2021 peak. Source: USTA 2026 report (link above).
- Pickleball's pro ranks are reportedly mostly former tennis players. Source: https://sgbonline.com (secondary). **Unverified.**

## Spending (avid players)

- Average annual spend: facility fees about $913, lessons about $688, tournament entries about $517, league fees about $402. Source: 2025 Pickleball Market Playbook (SFIA + DUPR, n=1,102 avid US players, Apr-Jun 2025), https://www.dupr.com/pickleball-state-of-the-market-consumer-habits-report
- 90% of those players plan to keep or raise equipment spending. Same source.
- Sample is avid players (over 90% play 2+ times/week), so it is not representative of all players. Equipment spend per player: **unverified**.
- Tennis lesson and equipment spend: **unverified** (not searched).

## Trends

- Court shortage: overcrowded courts are the top complaint (44.4% of avid players). Same DUPR source.
- Building enough courts to match the top-25% US cities would take more than 25,000 new courts, about $902M at $35k per outdoor court. Source: SFIA + Pickleheads "State of Pickleball" via https://www.sportstravelmagazine.com/sfia-and-pickleheads-release-participation-infrastructure-report and https://recmanagement.com/articles/153900/pickleball-needs-900-million-investment-keep-demand
- US pickleball court count: about 68,458. Source: https://www.pickleheads.com/guides/pickleball-statistics (database count, not audited)
- Retention: tennis retention rose to 80% (USTA, link above). Pickleball retention: **unverified**.
- Beginner dropout: **unverified** (not found in searches).
- Tech adoption (booking apps, DUPR ratings, video/CV tools): **unverified** (not searched). Pickleheads and DUPR both run rating and booking products; no adoption figure found.
- Rec-play boom and scheduling friction: DUPR survey cites scheduling conflicts and lack of similar-skill opponents as top barriers. Same DUPR source.

## So what for a webcam CV demo app

- Pickleball is the growth side (about +23% YoY) and its pain point is court time and scheduling, so a demo that shows shot or rally tracking for casual players fits the rec-play audience.
- Most new players are adults 35+ (tennis data), so target self-coaching for adults rather than youth or junior-style content.
- Lessons (about $688/yr avid spend) are the paid category a CV coach tool would compete with, so frame the demo as a cheaper practice aid, not a replacement for a pro.
